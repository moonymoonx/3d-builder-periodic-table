## 2. Audience Critique (Curious Non-Expert Perspective)

### First 5 Seconds & One Obvious Action
- **Huge leap over M5:** Warm paper, porcelain tiles, no legend wall, and a clear `"Tap one to look inside"` invitation will pass the 5-second clarity test immediately.
- **Two risks to fix now:**
  1. **Flat tiles can read as 2D on first glance.** Equal-height low tiles on pale paper risk looking like a tilted HTML table rather than tactile 3D. Give tiles real ceramic block thickness, crisp glazed specular highlights, deeper shaded sides (35–40%, not 28%), and gentle pointer parallax so 3D craft is *felt* in second 1.
  2. **Stiff opening copy.** *"Everything you have ever touched is built from these kinds of atom."* sounds slightly unnatural. Try: *"Everything you have ever touched is built from these 118 ingredients."*

### Is "Heat Iron Until It Glows" the Right Wow Moment?
- **Yes—it is a strong, memorable wow, *especially* because the bright museum stage dims into a dark forge (`--kiln`).** That bright-to-dark theatrical transition is something Ryan's permanently dark showroom cannot replicate.
- **Gap:** It sits **3 taps deep** on one specific element (Iron). Most curious adults will tap **Gold**, **Carbon**, **Mercury**, or **Uranium** first! Make the forge-dimming glow universal across all solid metals (with an effortless one-tap `"Heat it ▶"` play button), AND surface the **table-wide Heat Map** (melting and boiling all 118 tiles at once) right on the home screen as an instant 1-click wow before anyone dives into a single tile.

### Light "Porcelain & Ink" vs. Dark Showroom
- **Light wins decisively for this audience**—*if executed with high tactile contrast.* Dark WebGL + heavy bloom now looks generic and strains mobile eyes; warm paper + stoneware ceramic + editorial serif reads like Bartosz Ciechanowski or a Taschen museum book—calmer, smarter, and 60 fps on phones.
- **One dark exception needed:** **"Its light" (emission spectra)** should also dim the stage to `--kiln`, just like *Heat it*. Neon orange-red and sodium yellow glow lines look lifeless on bright cream paper; darkening the room makes light-emitting physics pop and unifies the visual grammar (*daylight = matter; dark room = light & fire*).

### Which Top-Level Lenses? What's Missing & What to Cut?
- **Top-level lenses:** Don't hide the **Heat Map** inside a dropdown or put `Discovered` ahead of physical wonder, and don't put `Grip on electrons` and `Electron pull` side by side without clear separation (they sound interchangeable to non-chemists). Top 6 should be: **Families (default) · Heat map (state at T) · Melting point · Heft (density) · Atom size · Grip on electrons**—and promote **Story Lenses** (*In your phone*, *Made in stars*, *In your body*) out of "Step 7: Later" into Step 4! We already have open/public-domain data (USGS GIP 167, ICRP/Wikipedia, Wikimedia CC BY-SA Johnson 2019). One story lens beats three extra physics plots on human interest.
- **Cut / simplify:** Don't put `3.5 million iron atoms` on the compact element card (3 crisp facts—state, melting point, litre weight—is cleaner); defer WebAudio sound until visual 3D polish is locked; keep `Electron pull`, `Discovery year`, and `Crustal abundance` under *"More lenses"*.

## 3. Top 10 Concrete Changes (Ranked by Impact on Feel)

1. **Deepen tile 3D presence on the daylight stage** (thicker ceramic bevels, 35–40% darker side walls, soft specular glaze highlight, subtle pointer parallax) — *prevents "Porcelain & Ink" from reading like a flat pastel HTML table in the first 2 seconds.*
2. **Pull Story Lenses (*In your phone* via public-domain USGS GIP 167 + *Made in stars* via Wikimedia CC BY-SA) up from "Step 7: Later" to Step 4** — *story lenses are the single strongest answer to "why should I care?" and beat Ryan on emotional resonance.*
3. **Expose the table-wide "Heat map" as a visible 1-tap lens chip on Home** — *delivers an instant, unforgettable 1-click wow (melting all 118 tiles at once) without requiring 3 taps into Iron.*
4. **Dim the room to `--kiln` in "Its light" (emission spectra) too, not just "Heat it"** — *makes neon/firework glow vivid and establishes one memorable visual rule: daylight for matter, dark room for light and fire.*
5. **Fix the Atom-view `3d` cloverleaf vs. `m = 0` (`3d_{z²}`) mismatch and `"fill 4 shells"` wording** — *ensures the 3D cloud on screen actually matches the plain-English caption beneath it.*
6. **Hand-write sourced 1-liners for the top ~30 elements people click first (H, C, O, Fe, Au, Hg, Ga, Na, W, U, Og, etc.) in the HERO slice** — *eliminates robotic template text on a visitor's very first clicks.*
7. **Replace the Lithium hook on the neutral *Atom size* lens (use Helium leaking through balloons instead) and fix gas density temperature consistency (0 °C vs 20 °C)** — *avoids an embarrassing contradiction when a sharp reader checks Lithium's size or Helium's weight.*
8. **Upgrade the four weak family names (`Mineral metals`, `Other metals`, `Half-metals`, `Noble gases`)** — *keeps the museum voice dignified for PhDs/adults while fixing the Aluminium/Bismuth inaccuracy.*
9. **Tighten the landing prompt to *"Everything you have ever touched is built from these 118 ingredients."* and add a one-tap `"Heat it ▶"` auto-play button on the forge screen** — *makes the opening line warmer and lets casual visitors watch iron glow, snap, melt, and boil effortlessly.*
10. **Trim top-level number lenses to the 5 most intuitive + Heat Map (moving *Electron pull* and *Discovered* to "More") and defer WebAudio sound until visuals pass the review gate** — *reduces cognitive clutter and focuses engineering craft on 60 fps visual beauty first.*
